Vacationist vs. Tricount: which is better for group travel?

Short answer: if all your group needs is to split costs — fast, free, no clutter — Tricount is excellent and genuinely popular across Europe for exactly that. If you're planning a trip, Vacationist covers the same expense splitting plus everything else the group needs to actually agree on: activity voting, a shared calendar, packing lists, and encrypted travel documents.

What Tricount does better

Honesty first:

  • Pure, focused simplicity. Tricount does one thing — split a shared pot of expenses — and does it with almost no friction. Create a "tricount," add expenses, done.
  • Multi-currency handling. Tricount's currency conversion is a genuine strength, built for trips that cross borders.
  • Non-member participants. Like Vacationist, Tricount lets people be added and settled up without necessarily needing the full app experience — a real, useful option it gets right.
  • Established and trusted, especially in Europe. Tricount has a long track record and heavy usage across DACH and the rest of Europe; your group may already know it.

What Vacationist does better

  • The whole trip, not just the pot of money. A group trip isn't just shared expenses — it's deciding where to go, what to do, and who's bringing what. Tricount stops at the ledger; Vacationist handles the decisions too, in the same app where the costs land.
  • Group voting that actually resolves debates. Tricount has no mechanism for a group to agree on an activity or venue — that conversation happens elsewhere (usually a chat that goes in circles). Vacationist's five-tier voting turns "does anyone actually want to do this?" into a clear answer.
  • Shared lists and recipes. The supermarket run for the villa, the camping meal plan — shared shopping and packing lists with recipe sync turn planning into one flow instead of a separate notes app.
  • Encrypted travel documents. Group bookings need passport details shared somewhere; Vacationist offers an AES-256 encrypted vault with revocable organizer access instead of a chat screenshot.
  • Group chat next to the plan. Coordination happens in the same app as the costs and the calendar, not scattered across a separate messaging thread.

The realistic recommendation

  • Keep Tricount if your group's only real need is splitting a shared pot of money — a dinner series, a shared apartment, a simple weekend where nothing else needs coordinating.
  • Use Vacationist for trips where expense splitting is one part of a bigger coordination problem: deciding what to do, keeping documents safe, and making sure everyone — including the friend who never checks the group chat — actually knows the plan.

If your group has been "using Tricount for the money" and still drowning in chat chaos for everything else, that chaos is exactly the gap Vacationist fills.

Frequently asked questions

Is Vacationist really free like Tricount?

Yes. The core app — voting, expenses, lists, chat — is free with no ads. A Pro tier adds more planning days per year and unlimited members for large groups.

Can people join without creating an account, like Tricount's non-member option?

Yes — Vacationist guests join via a single invite link with no account required, and can still vote, edit shared lists, and appear in expense splits, not just view a ledger.

Does Vacationist handle multiple currencies as well as Tricount?

Tricount's multi-currency support is more extensive today. If your group is splitting costs across several currencies with frequent conversion, that's currently Tricount's stronger ground.

Which app is better for a trip with activities, not just shared costs?

Vacationist — Tricount has no activity voting, shared calendar, or packing lists, so anything beyond the expense ledger still needs a separate app or chat thread.
