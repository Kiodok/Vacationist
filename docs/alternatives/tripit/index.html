The 6 best TripIt alternatives for group travel in 2026

TripIt is genuinely good at one thing: forward a confirmation email and it builds a clean itinerary automatically. So why look for an alternative? Usually because your trip isn't just one person's itinerary — it's a group that needs to decide things together and split what it costs, and TripIt was never built for either.

To be fair to TripIt first: if you're a frequent, mostly-solo traveler who wants flight alerts and a tidy timeline built from your inbox, it's still excellent at that, and nothing below matches its email parsing. The alternatives below exist for the other half of most trips: the group.

1. Vacationist — best for group coordination

(Our app — judge the claims on their merits.)

Vacationist is built around what TripIt treats as out of scope entirely: the group. Every suggestion gets voted on with a 5-tier system (from "must do" to "group blocker"), every cost gets split properly with live balances and settle-up, and shared shopping and packing lists sync in real time.

  • The differentiator: friends join by one invite link with no account required — and can still vote, edit lists, and be in expense splits. TripIt's sharing is read-only; real participation needs everyone to have their own account.
  • Money: TripIt has no expense splitting at all. Vacationist tracks who paid, who owes whom, and settles up automatically.
  • Also unique: encrypted travel document storage with revocable organizer access, in-app group chat, offline-first sync for everyone.
  • What it lacks vs. TripIt: no automatic itinerary building from forwarded confirmation emails, no flight delay alerts, no points tracking — activities and bookings are added manually or voted on by the group.
  • Price: free, no ads; optional Pro.
  • Best for: friend groups, families, bachelorette and stag crews — any trip where the coordination and the money are the hard part, not the inbox.

2. Wanderlog — best for itinerary research and maps

Wanderlog goes further than TripIt into planning: maps, place discovery, day-by-day itineraries you build rather than just import.

  • Strengths: excellent maps integration, route optimization, polished itinerary views, can also import bookings from email.
  • Weaknesses: collaboration is shallower than it looks, expense splitting is basic, offline access sits behind a paywall.
  • Best for: trips where the bottleneck is finding places, not agreeing on them. Full comparison: Vacationist vs. Wanderlog.

3. Kayak Trips — best free alternative for automatic itineraries

Kayak's trip organizer does the same core trick TripIt built its name on — forward confirmation emails, get an automatic itinerary — as a free feature inside an app most travelers already have.

  • Strengths: free, no separate app needed if you already use Kayak for booking, decent email parsing.
  • Weaknesses: no group features, no expense splitting, itinerary depth is thinner than TripIt's dedicated tools.
  • Best for: solo travelers who want TripIt's core trick without a subscription.

4. Tricount — best for pure expense splitting

If the group's actual problem is money, not the itinerary, Tricount is a fast, focused tool for exactly that.

  • Strengths: dead simple, strong multi-currency support, participants can join without a full account, popular across Europe.
  • Weaknesses: no itinerary, no voting, no lists — it's a ledger, not a trip planner. Full comparison: Vacationist vs. Tricount.
  • Best for: groups whose only shared problem is splitting a pot of costs.

5. Troupe — best for a group-first alternative with a similar shape

Troupe covers similar ground to Vacationist: polls, a shared itinerary, and expense splitting built for groups from the start, unlike TripIt's single-traveler design.

  • Strengths: built specifically for group trip coordination, not adapted from a solo-itinerary tool.
  • Weaknesses: feature depth varies by area — check current specifics directly. Full comparison: Vacationist vs. Troupe.
  • Best for: groups who want a dedicated group-trip app and are comparing options in that category directly.

6. Google Maps lists + Sheets — best zero-new-apps stack

The pragmatic default when nobody wants to install anything: shared Maps lists for places, a spreadsheet for money, the group chat for everything else.

  • Strengths: everyone already has it, zero learning curve.
  • Weaknesses: this is the chaos the other apps exist to fix — no voting, manual expense math, decisions buried in chat scrollback.
  • Best for: small, easygoing groups with simple trips and high chat tolerance.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best free TripIt alternative for a group trip?

Vacationist — the core app (voting, expenses, lists, chat, offline sync) is free with no ads, and unlike TripIt, it's built for the group to make decisions together, not just track one person's bookings.

Which alternative doesn't require everyone to create an account?

Vacationist is the only one on this list where the whole group can fully participate — vote, edit lists, appear in expense splits — via a single invite link with no sign-up.

Is there a TripIt alternative that splits expenses?

Vacationist handles this natively with custom splits, live balances, and settle-up. If expense splitting is the only thing you need, Tricount is a strong focused option too.

Is there a free alternative that still auto-builds an itinerary from email like TripIt?

Kayak Trips is the closest free match for that specific feature. Vacationist doesn't parse confirmation emails automatically — activities and bookings are added manually or voted on by the group.
