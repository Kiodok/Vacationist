# Alternatives to the apps you already know

If you're here, one of the well-known apps isn't quite fitting your group trip. These lists rank the real alternatives for each — honestly, including where the app you're leaving is still the better choice.

## Choose the app you're replacing

### [Best Splitwise alternatives →](/alternatives/splitwise/)

Seven apps for splitting travel costs, from focused ledgers to full trip planners. Common reasons people look elsewhere: the free tier's limits and ads, a friend who refuses to make an account, or the realization that a trip needs more than a ledger.

### [Best Wanderlog alternatives →](/alternatives/wanderlog/)

Six options that actually handle group coordination — voting, shared costs, guests without accounts — for people whose trip is more about the group than the map.

### [Best TripIt alternatives →](/alternatives/tripit/)

Six apps for groups, where TripIt's one-traveler, forwarded-email model doesn't fit: group decisions, shared costs, and people who won't create an account.

## How these lists work

Each list ranks its options for *group* trips specifically and says plainly where each app is weak. Vacationist appears in all three lists — usually first, because group coordination is the job it's built for — but the lists also name the cases where another app is the better pick. For a single app, see the head-to-head [comparison pages](/vs/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best free alternative for splitting group trip costs?

It depends on whether you need only a ledger or the whole trip. For a pure ledger, several focused apps exist and are covered in the [Splitwise alternatives list](/alternatives/splitwise/). For planning, voting and splitting in one place, Vacationist is free with no ads.

### Do I need everyone in the group to install the same app?

Not with Vacationist — guests can join a trip through a single link and take part without creating an account, and there is also a web app. Requirements differ between apps, which is one of the rows compared on each list.

### How are the alternatives chosen?

By what a group of friends or family actually needs on a trip: making decisions together, splitting costs fairly, keeping shared lists, and staying in sync. Each entry states its strengths and weaknesses rather than only its features.
